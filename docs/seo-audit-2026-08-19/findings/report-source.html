The short version

If you read nothing else, read this page.

Your site is in good shape. The plumbing most sites get wrong — titles, descriptions, the sitemap, redirects, alt text, security headers — is essentially flawless here. Across all 116 pages we found zero missing titles, zero duplicate titles or descriptions, zero images without alt text and zero broken structured data. That is rare. So this is not a list of errors — it is a list of twelve specific things holding the site back, most of them structural rather than broken. Two aren’t technical at all: they are claims the website makes about itself that don’t add up.

The biggest single risk: your site says it was founded this year

98 pages tell Google the clinic was founded in 2026, and two pages show visitors a stat card reading “2026 — Founded in Los Angeles.” Meanwhile your homepage headline claims 100+ Recoveries next to a Joint Commission seal and a state licence number.

A family researching treatment for someone they love will notice that a clinic founded eight months ago is claiming full accreditation and 100+ recoveries. That may be perfectly true — outpatient programmes move people through faster than residential, and accreditation can carry over — but the site never explains it. Google’s human quality raters are trained to look for exactly this kind of unexplained inconsistency on health sites. This is the first thing to fix.

The biggest single opportunity: your Spanish site is invisible

You built a complete Spanish version of the site — 44 pages. But the “Español” button in your navigation sends every visitor to one standalone page (/espanol) instead of to the Spanish version of the page they were reading. As a result, no English page on the site links into the Spanish section at all.

Google follows links to decide what matters. Your Spanish pages sit three to five clicks from the homepage and get about a third of the internal links your English pages get. Four of them — the Spanish FAQ, About, Our Story and Our Facility — have exactly one link pointing at them. In one of the most Spanish-speaking markets in America, that is a lot of work sitting unread. The fix is one line in a shared navigation file.

Fix now

Five things. Two are about trust, three are about being found. None of them require new content.

1

The site says it was founded this year

What’s wrong

The code on 98 pages tells Google foundingDate: "2026". Two pages — About Us and Our Story — show visitors a stat card that reads “2026 / Founded in Los Angeles,” and the Our Story page description says the clinic “was founded in 2026.” At the same time the homepage headline says 100+ Recoveries and the site displays Joint Commission accreditation and DHCS licence #191643AP.

Why it matters

Addiction treatment is what Google calls a “Your Money or Your Life” topic — the category it holds to the highest evidence standard, because bad information causes real harm. Google pays human reviewers to check whether a site’s claims about itself hold together. A clinic that says it opened eight months ago while claiming a mature track record and full accreditation is exactly the pattern they are trained to flag. Prospective clients notice too.

What to change

Pick one of two paths. Either state the real opening date and reconcile it in plain language on the About and Our Story pages — for example, “Since opening in [month] 2026, we’ve supported 100+ clients through treatment” — or, if 2026 was placeholder text that shipped by accident, correct it to the real year everywhere.

Do not simply delete the founding date and leave “100+ Recoveries” unexplained. That removes the evidence, not the question. The 100+ figure should stay in the headline; it is the founding side that needs fixing.

Effort: Half a day · One find-and-replace across 98 files, plus a short paragraph on two pages. Needs the real date from you before anyone can start.
2

Nowhere on the site can anyone read or leave a review

What’s wrong

We searched all 116 pages for testimonials, star ratings, review code, or a “leave us a review” link. There are none — not one. There is also no embedded Google map anywhere on the public site. Meanwhile three real Google review screenshots dated 27 July sit unused in a folder in the project.

Why it matters

For a local health service, review volume and recency are among the strongest factors in whether you appear in the Google map results — and AI assistants like ChatGPT and Google’s AI Overviews lean heavily on review text when someone asks “is this place any good.” Right now the site gives them nothing to work with, and gives a satisfied client no way to help you.

What to change

Three steps, in this order. First, add a “Leave us a review” link pointing at your Google review form — footer or alumni page. There is nothing to write and no compliance question; do it this week. Second, embed a real Google map on the Contact and Locations pages. Third, decide with whoever handles compliance whether to display consented testimonials with a live star rating.

Important: do not publish those screenshots as page content or mark them up as review data. A review of an addiction treatment centre names a person’s health condition, so displaying one needs explicit written consent from the reviewer.

Effort: Step one is 15 minutes. Steps two and three are half a day plus a compliance decision.
3

The business information you send Google contradicts itself

What’s wrong

Every page carries a hidden block of code describing the business — name, address, phone, service area, credentials. It is meant to be one identical description repeated everywhere. Instead it was copy-pasted and the copies have quietly drifted apart:

  • The service area is 7 cities on the homepage, 12 on the neighbourhood pages, and 6 on the Spanish homepage.
  • The business logo is one file everywhere except the 11 neighbourhood pages, which point at a different image.
  • The list of specialities is 9 items in English and only 4 in Spanish.
  • The founding date appears on some pages and is missing from others.
Why it matters

All of these copies carry the same identifier, so Google treats them as one business. When one business tells Google three different service areas and two different logos, Google has to guess which is right — and confidence in the whole record drops. This is the data that feeds your map listing and knowledge panel.

What to change

Stop copy-pasting it. Generate the business block once from a single source file and inject the identical text everywhere, or define it fully on one page and have every other page point back to that definition. Either approach makes drift impossible going forward. Corrected code is ready to paste in the technical findings.

Effort: Half a day. Also fixes a related gap — the business record never lists your actual programmes (PHP, IOP, telehealth), so nothing tells Google what you do without reading the prose.
4

Your Spanish site is cut off from your English site

What’s wrong

The “Español” link in the navigation goes to /espanol on every single English page — a standalone Spanish landing page — rather than to the Spanish version of the page the visitor is on. But in the page code, you correctly tell Google that /es/… holds the Spanish version. The navigation and the code disagree.

The result: of the 71 pages outside the Spanish section, only the 404 page links into it. Spanish pages sit three to five clicks deep and get about a third of the internal links English pages get. The Spanish FAQ, About, Our Story and Our Facility pages have one inbound link each.

Why it matters

Internal links are how Google decides which of your pages deserve attention. Pages buried five clicks deep with one link pointing at them get crawled rarely and rank poorly. You have 44 well-built Spanish pages doing almost nothing, in a market where a large share of families searching for treatment search in Spanish.

What to change

Change the language switcher so it sends someone reading /treatments/fentanyl to /es/treatments/fentanyl, falling back to the Spanish homepage when no translation exists. Keep /espanol as a campaign landing page, reachable from the Spanish navigation. Nothing else needs to change — the language tags are already correct.

Effort: Half a day. It lives in one shared navigation component.
5

An icon library is 94% of your JavaScript — and it makes the page jump

What’s wrong

Every page loads a 94 KB icon library from an outside server called unpkg. Your own code is about 6 KB. The library arrives late, then swaps 49 empty placeholders on the homepage into 24-pixel icons — after the page has already drawn. Nothing in the stylesheet reserves space for them, so content visibly shifts as they appear, including the “Call Now” button in the header.

Why it matters

Content jumping around after load is one of the three page-experience signals Google measures directly, and it is the one visitors feel most — it is what makes someone tap the wrong thing. On top of that, you are trusting an outside server: if unpkg has an outage, every icon on your site disappears.

What to change

Today, one line: add a stylesheet rule reserving 24×24 pixels for every icon placeholder. That stops the jumping immediately. Then, properly: you use roughly 15–20 unique icons. Paste those directly into the pages at build time and delete the library. That removes 94 KB, an outside dependency, and the shift, all at once.

Effort: The stopgap is one line and ten minutes. The full fix is half a day.

Fix this week

Four changes that are small in effort and disproportionate in effect.

6

Six pages load a hero image four times bigger than they need

What’s wrong

The Team, Our Facility and Our Story pages — and their three Spanish versions — build their hero image in a way the browser can’t discover early, and none of the six tells the browser to start loading it. They also point at the full-size file when a smaller version is already built and deployed right beside it:

  • Team page loads waiting-area.webp at 217 KB — waiting-area-800.webp is 53 KB.
  • Another page loads a 142 KB file where a 42 KB version exists.

The other 70 pages with hero images already do this correctly.

Why it matters

The hero image is usually the largest thing on screen, and Google measures how long it takes to appear. These six pages are four times slower than they need to be, on files you have already paid to create.

What to change

Add one preload line to each of the six pages and point it at the smaller variant on phone-sized screens.

Effort: Under an hour. This is the cheapest real win in the report.
7

Blog posts look two months out of date to readers and to AI

What’s wrong

Every treatment, programme and location page carries a visible “Updated [Month Year]” line under the medical reviewer’s name. Not one of the 16 blog posts does. Several show a June publish date on the page while the hidden code says they were reviewed on 19 August.

Why it matters

AI assistants and readers both go by what they can see. A cost guide that looks like it was written in June reads as stale next to a competitor’s that says “updated this month” — even though yours actually was.

What to change

Add the same visible “Updated” line to all 16 posts, matching the date already in the code.

Effort: Under an hour.
8

Your headings don’t match the questions people actually ask

What’s wrong

Only 11% of the section headings across the site are phrased as questions, and nearly all of those are inside FAQ dropdowns. The headings that sit above your actual answers are labels or slogans — “Fentanyl Is Different, So Treatment Has to Be” instead of “How is fentanyl addiction treatment different?”. Several sections also open with a rhetorical lead-in rather than the answer.

Why it matters

When ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google’s AI Overview answers a question, it lifts a self-contained passage that sits directly under a matching heading. A slogan heading followed by a scene-setting paragraph gives them nothing liftable. Your Santa Monica page shows how good this can be — its FAQ answers give exact drive times, roads and bus lines, and scored 9 out of 10 for how quotable they are.

What to change

Rewrite six specific headings into question form and move the direct answer into the first sentence beneath each. The exact rewrites are listed in the technical findings.

Effort: Half a day, concentrated on the cost and insurance posts and the fentanyl page.

10

Get listed where AI assistants look for treatment centres

What’s wrong

There is no sign anywhere on the site that you are listed on Psychology Today, Healthgrades, the Better Business Bureau, or — most importantly — SAMHSA’s FindTreatment.gov provider directory. Your site links out to SAMHSA twelve times as a resource, which is not the same as being in it.

Why it matters

When someone asks an AI assistant for a rehab near them, it leans hard on government and high-authority directories. Being absent from FindTreatment.gov means being absent from a large share of those answers. Psychology Today carries similar weight for behavioural health specifically.

One thing you already do right: LegitScript certification — the one Google requires before a treatment centre can advertise — is displayed and linked for verification.

What to change

Claim each listing, then add the live profile links to your site’s business record so Google can connect them to you.

Effort: Multi-day, mostly waiting on verification. Start now.

12

The remaining speed and structure work

What to change
  • Build smaller hero images. The main hero photos have no phone-sized versions, so a mobile visitor downloads the same file a desktop does — around 100 KB on most pages. Affects roughly 70 pages.
  • Host your fonts yourself. Fonts come from Google’s servers through two separate round trips before any text can display in its final form. Self-hosting removes both and stops the headline reflowing as the font loads.
  • Fix the heading structure on 48 pages. They jump from a section heading straight to a sub-sub-heading, skipping a level. Mainly an accessibility issue — it affects screen readers more than rankings — and it lives in a couple of shared components.
  • Finish or remove the amenity map page. It is live, publicly linked from four pages, and still contains unfilled template placeholders like {{website}}. It also loads two large outside libraries.
  • Vary the repeated blocks on the neighbourhood pages. Good news first: these pages are not thin copies of each other. Real driving routes, real bus lines, real local framing — we measured it and they genuinely differ. But the “Levels of Care” and “Why Golden State Rehab” card blocks repeat across all 11 with only cosmetic rewording. Vary them before adding a twelfth neighbourhood page, not after.
Effort: Several days across the group. None of it is urgent.

One security item to check outside the website

The amenity map page has a Google Maps key visible in its source code. Keys like this are public by design, so this is not a leak — but it must have website restrictions applied in the Google Cloud console, otherwise anyone can run up charges on your account. Worth five minutes to confirm.

The neighbourhood pages pass the test that matters

Eleven pages targeting eleven neighbourhoods, for a business with one office, is the classic setup for a Google penalty. Yours avoid it. Swapping “Santa Monica” for “Venice” would produce factually wrong sentences, which is the test. The code also correctly describes one business serving a service area, rather than inventing eleven fake offices. Don’t change that part.

What we could not measure

Stated plainly so no number in this report gets more weight than it has earned.

The speed score is the weakest number in this report

Google’s free speed-testing API returned a quota error, and its real-world performance database requires an API key we don’t have. That means no actual loading-speed measurements were taken. Everything in the speed section comes from measuring file sizes and reading the code — the problems identified are real and verified in the source, but the score of 76 is an informed estimate, not a measurement.

Before acting on any speed number here, get a real one. The tooling to do it is already installed on the machine and needs no account or quota — one command produces a full report in about a minute.

Also outside what a website audit can see:

  • Your backlink profile. Who links to you, and whether any of it is harmful, is unmeasured.

Two corrections to last week’s audit

The insurance logo sizes are not causing layout shift. Last week’s report flagged 980 image tags with wrong dimensions as a page-jumping problem. Reading the stylesheet shows it overrides those dimensions before anything is drawn, so no shift occurs. The dimensions are still factually wrong and worth correcting, but this is a tidiness item, not a speed one — it should not be on anyone’s urgent list.

The language script should not be deferred. Last week’s fix for a blocking script was to defer it. That would break the feature — the script deliberately runs before the page draws to stop Spanish-speaking visitors seeing a flash of English first. The right fix is to paste its 2 KB directly into the page instead of fetching it as a separate file.
