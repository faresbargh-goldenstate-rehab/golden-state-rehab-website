A Program, Not Just Appointments

What Our Alcoholism Treatment Program Actually Is

An alcoholism treatment program is different from "seeing a therapist about drinking." It's a structured pathway: a set number of clinical hours each week, a primary therapist who owns your case, group therapy built around alcohol recovery, psychiatric support when your plan calls for it, and a deliberate taper as you get stronger. At Golden State Rehab, that pathway runs entirely on an outpatient basis at our Westwood center — you get real clinical structure during the day or evening, and you sleep in your own bed.

To understand alcohol addiction itself — signs, effects, withdrawal — see our alcohol addiction treatment page. This page covers the program: how it's structured, what the weeks look like, and how you move through it.

The program fits people across a wide range of situations — the professional whose drinking has quietly escalated, the parent who's tried to cut back alone and can't, the person leaving a detox or residential stay who needs structure before standing fully on their own. Your intake assessment determines where you start; everything after that is adjusted to how you're actually doing, not a preset calendar.

Levels & Components

How the Program Is Structured

PHP Track

Our most structured level: full weekdays of clinical treatment while you live at home. The PHP program is where many people begin when drinking has destabilized daily life — maximum support up front, then a step down as you stabilize.

IOP Track

3-hour sessions, 3–5 days a week, in a morning or evening track. IOP is the program's workhorse level — enough structure to hold recovery steady while you keep working. The evening IOP exists specifically for people who can't step away from a job.

Telehealth Option

The same program, delivered through secure video anywhere in California. Join group and individual sessions from home — useful when commuting to Westwood is the thing that would keep you from showing up consistently.

Weekly Individual Therapy

At least one private session a week with your primary therapist, at every level of the program. Individual therapy is where the work gets specific: the situations you drink in, the history underneath it, and the plan for the week ahead.

Group Therapy

The backbone of PHP and IOP alike. Groups are led by licensed clinicians and filled with people fighting the same fight — which matters, because alcohol recovery in isolation is exactly how most people got stuck in the first place.

Medication Support

When clinically appropriate, our psychiatric team may include anti-craving medications such as naltrexone as part of a treatment plan, with ongoing medication management alongside therapy. Not everyone needs medication — the decision is made with your prescriber, not by default.

The Arc of Treatment

What the Weeks Look Like

Every person's timeline is their own, but the program follows a recognizable arc — structure that's heaviest at the start and deliberately loosens as your recovery strengthens:

  • Stabilization. The early weeks are about steadying the ground: consistent attendance, honest assessment of where drinking fits in your life, and getting sleep, mood, and routine back under you — with psychiatric support if your plan calls for it.
  • Skills and relapse prevention. The core of the program: identifying your specific triggers, building refusal and coping skills in group, and doing the deeper individual work on what alcohol has been solving for you.
  • Step-down. As you stabilize, hours taper — PHP to IOP, IOP to fewer days, then outpatient rehab — so support decreases gradually as real life takes more of the weight, instead of ending all at once.
  • Alumni and aftercare. Treatment ends; support doesn't. Our alumni program keeps you connected with check-ins, events, and a community that knows exactly what early sobriety asks of you.

Where appropriate, family members are brought into the process — recovery goes better when the people you live with understand what you're doing and how to support it. And throughout, the focus stays on the life you're returning to: work, relationships, and weekends that don't revolve around drinking.
