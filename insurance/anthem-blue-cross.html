Using Anthem for Treatment

California's Blue Cross Plan, Explained

Anthem Blue Cross holds the Blue Cross license in California and is part of Elevance Health, one of the country's largest insurers — which means millions of Californians carry an Anthem card through work, Covered California, or an individual plan. One point of frequent confusion: Anthem Blue Cross and Blue Shield of California are separate, unaffiliated companies, so check which name is actually on your card before comparing benefits. On Anthem plans, behavioral health benefits are typically administered through Carelon Behavioral Health.

Under the federal Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act and California's SB 855, a plan that covers medical care must cover medically necessary addiction and mental health treatment on comparable terms. How that translates into dollars depends on your specific plan, which is why our admissions team verifies Anthem benefits for free — usually within an hour — and walks you through what your plan may pay toward outpatient rehab in Los Angeles. No obligation, and Anthem is never told you asked.

Levels of Care

Levels of Care Anthem Plans May Help Fund

Partial Hospitalization (PHP)

Our highest level of outpatient structure — full treatment days, home every night. Anthem plans typically route PHP requests through Carelon for prior authorization, a clinical review our team assembles and submits. Read about PHP in Los Angeles.

Intensive Outpatient (IOP)

Three-hour sessions, morning or evening, three to five days a week. IOP fits around a job and, for many Anthem members, is the level authorized with the least back-and-forth. See how our intensive outpatient program is structured.

Outpatient & Telehealth

Weekly therapy and psychiatry, in person in Westwood or through secure telehealth anywhere in California. Anthem plans have generally extended behavioral health benefits to virtual visits on par with office visits.

What Affects Your Coverage

What Actually Determines Your Anthem Out-of-Pocket Cost

  • PPO vs. HMO/EPO. Anthem PPO plans usually include out-of-network benefits; HMO and EPO plans typically pay only for in-network care. Your card's plan type is the first thing we look at.
  • Where your deductible stands. The same plan costs you very different amounts in January than in October. We confirm what you've already met this year.
  • Your out-of-pocket maximum. Every plan caps your annual spending on covered care — if surgery or other bills already pushed you near it, treatment may cost less than you fear.
  • Carelon authorization. PHP and IOP typically need prior authorization through Carelon Behavioral Health, plus periodic reviews during treatment. We handle both.
  • Medical necessity. Benefits follow the level of care your clinical assessment supports — so a free, no-pressure assessment is where the process starts.
