Using UnitedHealthcare for Treatment

Big Insurer, Real Behavioral Health Benefits

UnitedHealthcare is part of UnitedHealth Group, the largest health insurer in the United States, and nearly all of its commercial plans come with behavioral health benefits attached. Here's the detail most members don't know: those benefits are typically administered by Optum Behavioral Health, a sister company, which runs its own provider network and its own authorization process. Federal parity law and California's SB 855 still apply — medically necessary addiction and mental health treatment must be covered on terms comparable to medical care.

The practical questions are which plan you hold — Choice, Choice Plus, Navigate, Compass, or another — where your deductible stands, and what Optum wants authorized first. Rather than have you sit on hold, our admissions team verifies your UnitedHealthcare benefits for free, usually within an hour, and lays out what your plan may pay for outpatient rehab in Los Angeles. Nothing is reported back to UnitedHealthcare about why you asked, and there's no obligation.

What Affects Your Coverage

The Variables Behind Your UnitedHealthcare Numbers

  • Which UHC plan you hold. Choice Plus is PPO-style and usually includes out-of-network benefits; Choice, Navigate, and Compass plans generally keep you inside their networks. The plan name is printed on your card.
  • Optum's role. Behavioral health claims and authorizations typically run through Optum Behavioral Health, not UnitedHealthcare's medical side — a distinction that changes who approves what.
  • Deductible progress. If doctor visits or prescriptions have already chipped away at your deductible this year, your share of treatment costs may be smaller than you fear.
  • Out-of-pocket maximum. Once you hit this annual ceiling, cost-sharing on covered services generally stops for the year.
  • Prior authorization and medical necessity. PHP and IOP typically need Optum's approval, tied to your clinical assessment — which is why admissions begins with a free assessment.
