Using Blue Shield for Treatment

A California Plan, Built for Californians

Blue Shield of California is an independent nonprofit health plan that serves only California — and it's a separate company from Anthem Blue Cross, even though the names sound alike, so check your card carefully before comparing benefits. Because every Blue Shield plan is a California plan, state protections apply broadly: SB 855 requires coverage of medically necessary substance use and mental health treatment, and the federal parity law requires those benefits to be comparable to medical benefits.

One quirk worth knowing: Blue Shield typically administers behavioral health benefits through its mental health service administrator rather than its medical line, so the phone number for those benefits may differ from the one on the front of your card. We handle that maze for you. Our admissions team verifies your Blue Shield benefits for free — usually within an hour — and translates the deductibles and authorization rules into plain numbers for outpatient rehab in Los Angeles. Blue Shield is never told why you asked.

Levels of Care

Care Blue Shield Plans May Help Pay For

Partial Hospitalization (PHP)

Structured treatment five or six hours a day, weekdays, while you live at home. Blue Shield plans generally require prior authorization for PHP through the behavioral health administrator — a step our team manages start to finish. See what PHP in Los Angeles involves.

Intensive Outpatient (IOP)

Three-hour blocks, several days a week, with tracks before and after work. For many Blue Shield members, IOP is the level of care that fits both their clinical needs and their plan's rules. Explore our intensive outpatient program.

Outpatient & Telehealth

Ongoing therapy, psychiatry, and telehealth sessions available across the state. California law generally requires plans like Blue Shield to reimburse covered telehealth behavioral care on the same basis as in-person care.

What Affects Your Coverage

What Actually Determines Your Blue Shield Costs

  • PPO vs. HMO or Trio. Blue Shield PPO plans usually include out-of-network benefits; HMO and Trio HMO plans typically pay only within their network. Your plan type is on your member card.
  • Who administers behavioral health. Many Blue Shield plans route mental health and substance use benefits through a mental health service administrator with its own network and authorization process.
  • Deductible and coinsurance. What you've already spent this year matters as much as the plan's headline numbers — a met deductible can dramatically lower your share.
  • Prior authorization. PHP and IOP typically require sign-off before treatment begins, plus ongoing clinical reviews. We submit and track all of it.
  • Medical necessity. Benefits apply at the level of care your assessment supports, which is why every admission starts with a free clinical assessment.
