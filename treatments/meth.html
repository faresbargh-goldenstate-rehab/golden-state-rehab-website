What is meth addiction treatment?

It's outpatient care for methamphetamine use disorder, the medical name for meth addiction. Meth changes the brain's dopamine system, and the changes from long-term use take time and support to heal. There's no FDA-approved medicine for meth, so behavioral therapy is the main treatment. You come to our Westwood center for PHP or IOP and sleep at home.

Source: NIDA methamphetamine research report.

What if part of me doesn't want to quit?

That's common with meth, and you can still start. Motivational interviewing, or MI, is a kind of talk therapy that helps you weigh what meth gives you against what you want for your life. You don't have to be sure on day one.

  • You've tried to stop on your own and ended up using again.
  • You feel flat and empty when you're not using.
  • You've noticed paranoia or dark moods creeping in.
  • You want your energy and your focus back, even if quitting feels scary.

Call before you feel sure

How do you treat meth without medication?

With behavioral therapy, which research shows works for meth. One of the strongest tools is contingency management, part of the Matrix Model. That's an intensive outpatient approach developed in Los Angeles for stimulant recovery. Many people recover with the right structure around them.

Source: NIDA, effective treatments for meth use.

Contingency management

You earn rewards for drug-free urine tests and for showing up to treatment. Early wins start to stack up.

CBT

Cognitive behavioral therapy helps you spot your triggers and build a plan for cravings. You learn ways through the emotional lows of early recovery.

Exercise

Movement is one of the few things with evidence for helping the brain recover from meth. It helps restore the dopamine meth has used up.

Body and brain care

Yoga, breathwork, nutrition and sleep habits help bring back your baseline mood and energy.

How long until I feel normal again?

It takes months, and the hardest part comes early. For many people, weeks 2 to 4 are the toughest stretch. After the first month, mood and energy slowly come back as the brain heals. Knowing what's coming makes the early weeks easier to get through.

Everyone heals at their own pace, so treat this as a common pattern.

  1. Week 1

    The crash

    Deep tiredness and low mood. Your appetite goes up.

  2. Weeks 2 to 4

    The gray period

    Low mood and trouble feeling pleasure. Hang on untill it lifts, because it does.

  3. Months 1 to 3

    A slow lift

    Mood and energy start to return, and thinking gets clearer.

  4. Months 3 to 6

    Brain healing

    Sleep improves, and everyday things start to feel good again.

  5. 6+ months

    Your new normal

    Healing keeps going while you build a life that doesn't revolve around meth.

Can you help with the paranoia and depression too?

Yes. Paranoia and depression often show up in meth recovery, and some people have psychosis caused by meth. Our medical team treats these through dual diagnosis care, so your mental health and your meth use get treated together. Behavioral therapy still does the core work of recovery.

Do I start in PHP or IOP?

Your free assessment decides. Many people start in PHP, about 6 hours a day on weekdays, to get through the hardest early weeks with support. As the brain heals and routines settle, they step down to IOP and then alumni support.

  1. Free assessment

    By phone or in person. It sets your starting level.

  2. PHP

    About 6 hours a day, Monday to Friday. You go home each night.

  3. IOP

    3-hour sessions, 3 to 5 days a week, in a morning or evening track.

  4. Alumni

    A year of weekly alumni groups, plus a check-in every month.

Will insurance pay for meth rehab?

Your plan may cover it. We work with most major PPO plans, and checking your benefits is free and confidential. Federal parity law and California's SB 855 say health plans must cover addiction treatment that's medically necessary.
