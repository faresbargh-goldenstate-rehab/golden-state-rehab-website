A Way of Delivering Care — Not Just Another Technique

What Does "Trauma-Informed" Actually Mean?

Trauma-informed therapy is not a single technique. It's a way of delivering all care — every session, every group, every interaction with staff — built on one assumption: trauma may be part of your story, whether or not you've ever named it that way. A trauma-informed program is designed so that treatment itself never re-traumatizes you. In practice, that means five commitments woven through everything we do: safety first, trust and transparency, collaboration, choice, and empowerment.

Why does this matter so much in addiction treatment? Because substance use is very often a response to unprocessed trauma — the most reliable relief a person could find for pain nobody helped them carry. Programs that treat the drinking or the using while ignoring what's underneath tend to watch the cycle repeat: get sober, get flooded, relapse. Trauma-informed care treats both — the substance use and the pain driving it — so recovery has something solid to stand on.

At Golden State Rehab in Westwood, trauma-informed principles shape our entire outpatient program — including specialized care for PTSD and complex trauma, and addiction treatment that takes your history seriously instead of treating it as a footnote.

How It Works

The Principles in Practice

Trauma-informed care is easy to claim and hard to do. Here's what it actually looks like inside our Los Angeles program — in the room, in your treatment plan, and in how our team works with you.

Physical & Emotional Safety

Before any deeper work happens, you need to feel safe — in the building, in the group room, with your therapist. We build predictable routines, clear expectations, and a calm environment so your nervous system can finally stand down.

You Control the Pace

Nobody at Golden State Rehab is forced to retell their story before they're ready. You decide what you share, when, and with whom. Healing doesn't require reliving everything on someone else's schedule — and pushing too fast can do real harm.

Trust & Transparency

You always know what's happening in your treatment and why. Your clinicians explain the reasoning behind every recommendation, and nothing about your care is decided behind closed doors. Trauma thrives in secrecy; recovery doesn't.

Collaboration, Not Compliance

Your treatment plan is made with you, not handed to you. Trauma often involves having power taken away — so a trauma-informed program returns it, treating you as the expert on your own life and your clinicians as partners.

Skills Before Processing

Stabilization comes first: grounding, emotion regulation, and coping skills that keep you steady in daily life. Direct trauma processing only begins when you have the tools to do it safely — and only when you're ready.

Integrated With CBT & DBT

Trauma-informed care is the frame; evidence-based therapies are what happens inside it. We deliver CBT and DBT through a trauma-informed lens — the same proven methods, paced and adapted to your history.

Treating the Root

Trauma and Addiction: Treating the Root

For many people, substances were never the real problem — they were the solution that eventually became one. Alcohol quiets hypervigilance. Opioids numb what feels unbearable. Stimulants push away the emptiness. When treatment removes the substance without addressing the pain it was managing, relapse isn't a moral failure — it's a predictable outcome.

That's why trauma-informed care runs through every layer of our outpatient programs: individual therapy, where the deeper one-on-one work happens at your pace; group therapy, where connection replaces the isolation trauma creates; and psychiatric care, where medication decisions account for your full history — not just your symptoms this week.

Trauma-informed therapy may be right for you if:

  • You have a history of PTSD or complex trauma — diagnosed or not
  • You experienced childhood trauma, neglect, or an unsafe home
  • Your relapses tend to follow triggers, anniversaries, or reminders
  • You've felt unsafe, unheard, or pushed too hard in past treatment settings
  • You live with co-occurring anxiety or depression alongside substance use
